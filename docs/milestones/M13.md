# M13: Scaling (H2)

Status: **complete and approved by the owner (2026-10-16)** (branch `m11-complete`). Design: `docs/PREREGISTRATION.md`, Amendments 3, 6 and 7. Every number comes from `results/scaling/`.

## What was built

- **Scaling data** (`scripts/simulate_scaling.sh`, manifest `data/manifests/simulated/scaling.tsv`): AliSim genomes of 1,000,000 letters on random Yule–Harding trees, m = 25, 50, 100 (3 replicates each) and m = 200 (1 replicate, not run; Amendment 7).
- **Queues** (outside the repository, `D:\_external\queues\`): `m13_queue.sh` runs Q-MAWS (`--seed 1 --bootstrap 0`) and ML-MAWS (`--strand --iqtree`, all cores) one at a time; `m13_mlmaws_complete.sh` completes ML-MAWS above 50 taxa (Amendment 6); `unthrottle.ps1` keeps ML-MAWS and IQ-TREE out of Windows' efficiency mode, as Q-MAWS already does for itself; `m13_memory.ps1` and `m13_memory2.ps1` record peak memory.
- **Evaluation** (`crates/qmaws-cli/src/h2_cmd.rs`, hidden `qmaws h2`): times per dataset (Q-MAWS: the larger of its summed attempts and its engine time; ML-MAWS: its last successful attempt, above 50 taxa only the rows of Amendment 6), the pre-registered criterion, the crossover, peak memory and `scaling.svg`.

## Acceptance criterion

| Criterion | Result |
|---|---|
| H2 evaluated as pre-registered, or marked "not tested" with the reason | Done: **H2 is not supported** (below); the 200-taxa replicate is not tested, with the reason (Amendment 7) |

## Result

| m | Q-MAWS (s) | ML-MAWS (s) | Faster |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 43, 45, 44 | 1,383, 1,210, 1,322 | Q-MAWS, about 30× |
| 50 | 587, 586, 597 | 7,038, 3,143, 3,027 | Q-MAWS, about 5–12× |
| 100 | 11,616, 10,133, 13,218 | 1,751, 5,150, 1,513 | ML-MAWS, about 2–9× |

**H2 is not supported:** ML-MAWS was faster in all three replicates with m = 100 (criterion: Q-MAWS faster in every replicate with m ≥ 100). There is no crossover in Q-MAWS's favour. In plain words: Q-MAWS is much faster for small and medium datasets, but its time grows with the number of quartets (about m⁴), while ML-MAWS works on at most 50,000 columns; by 100 taxa ML-MAWS is faster.

Peak memory at m = 100: Q-MAWS about 0.75 GB, ML-MAWS about 2.2–3.0 GB. At m = 200 Q-MAWS would need about 12 GB in its amalgamation stage.

## How it was verified

- Every Q-MAWS run passed `qmaws verify --quick` (reports in each run's `report/verify_*.txt`).
- The H2 command has eight unit tests (criterion, crossover, resumed attempts, attempts without a row, the Amendment 6 rule, memory rows, the figure).

## How the runs were made

M13 started on its own when H5 finished (2026-10-14 19:11 UTC) and ended on 2026-10-15 20:57 UTC. Two laptop shutdowns stopped Q-MAWS runs at m = 100; they resumed from their saved chunks (the engine time covers every attempt). The ML-MAWS runs above 50 taxa ran after the queue, one at a time, under Amendment 6. Details and caveats (the failed fast-mode rows, timing variability of ML-MAWS, about 25 minutes of light work during one m = 25 ML-MAWS run, the gap of the first memory recorder) are in `results/scaling/README.md`.

## Known limitations

- Three replicates per size on one laptop; ML-MAWS's run times vary between replicates (IQ-TREE's search length differs).
- Q-MAWS's memory limits it to about 150 taxa on an 8 GB computer (estimate from the measured 2.97 weighted quartets per quartet and the M7 memory per weighted quartet).
- ML-MAWS above 50 taxa was completed with the IQ-TREE command of its normal mode; as released it does not finish there.
